**Elysia** (/ɪˈliːʒə/, *ih-LEE-zhuh*) is [[avium]]’s fourth-most populated and third-largest continent. In general terms, it is bounded on the north by the Arctic Sea and the Hyperborean Ocean, on the west and south by the Western Panthalassan Ocean, and on the east by the Sargassum Straits, the Sahara Sea, and the Sea of [[auritania]].

## Etymology

‘Elysia’ derives from the ancient Astriyan *Elysion* or Elysian Fields, the paradise of the blessed in Astriyan mythology, where heroes and the virtuous were said to dwell after death. Rumaic explorers and colonists, sailing westward from the Barbary across the Sargassum Straits between 50 BCE and 50 CE, encountered the Eastern Elysian coastline and named the continent after the mythological realm due to the perceived abundance and fertility of the land compared to the more densely settled regions of [[evria]]. The Rumaic adaptation, *Elysia*, gradually became standardized in international cartography following sustained periods of colonization. Indigenous populations had their own names for the continent, but Rumaic geographic nomenclature became the internationally recognized standard.

## History

Prior to Evrian contact, the Elysian continent supported diverse indigenous populations numbering in the millions, distributed across complex societies that spanned from hunter-gatherer bands to urbanized agricultural civilizations. These peoples, collectively termed Native Elysians in later Evrian literature, developed distinct cultural, linguistic, and political systems adapted to the continent's varied geography and climates. Archaeological evidence suggests continuous habitation dating back millennia, characterized by sophisticated trade networks, monumental architecture, and advanced agricultural techniques documented across multiple regions.

### Rumaic Colonization

Beginning around 1000 BCE, Thalassan sailors on long-range maritime expeditions began reaching the Eastern Elysian coast intermittently. They established sporadic trade contacts with coastal indigenous populations (e.g., Lamanicks, Frishans, Thrams, Quinars), but founded no permanent settlements, viewing the distant continent primarily as a source of exotic goods. Knowledge of Elysia's existence circulated within Thalassan commercial networks but remained largely confined to maritime trading communities, unknown to the broader populations of Evria.

The Rumaic Empire expressed its interest in Elysia around 50 BCE. Rumaic explorers, having acquired geographic knowledge from Thalassan sources, mounted expeditions to establish permanent settlements along the northeastern Elysian seaboard, collectively known as the **Territoria Elysiaca**. The colony served as an agricultural producer, supplying grain and timber to Evria, and as a strategic outpost, securing Rumaic maritime dominance in the Sargassum Straits and the Sahara Sea. Unlike the Thalassans, Rumaic settlers imported their own legal systems, urban planning, infrastructure construction, and provincial administration.

Rumaic colonial expansion progressed steadily westward from the coast over four centuries, establishing a network of fortified towns, agricultural estates, and road systems that extended through the coastal plain and into the interior uplands. By the early 400s CE, Rumaic authority reached its maximum extent, encompassing territories from the Saharan seaboard, west through the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes), and south roughly along the [Patawomeck River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potomac_River). This expansion displaced or subjugated numerous indigenous polities; however, Rumaic control remained concentrated in the lowlands and major river valleys, with the highland and forested regions often remaining under indigenous authority or in a state of contestation.

### Triumvirate Period

The fall of the Rumaic Empire in 476 caused immediate political fragmentation across the Territoria Elysiaca. With no functional authority to confer legitimacy or coordinate, three major successor states emerged, each claiming to continue Rumaic legal and administrative traditions while establishing its own independent government.

The most powerful successor state, adopting the name **Elysia** for itself, consolidated control over the oldest territories in the original colony. It maintained the most orthodox adherence to Rumaic legal codes and administrative practices, positioning itself as the legitimate continuation of Rumaic authority on the continent. The state's claims to precedence rested on its control of the most economically developed colonial regions, though its actual power remained confined to coastal lowlands.

**Varennia** emerged north of the [Magtogoek River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lawrence_River) under the leadership of a powerful military commander who had controlled the northwestern frontier garrisons. Varennia established a more militarized government structure than its rivals, organized around a standing army necessary to maintain control over indigenous polities and to contest territorial claims with neighboring successor states.

**Interlacria** formed in the Great Lakes region, governed by an aristocratic council of major landowners. It controlled the most productive agricultural lands in the former Territoria Elysiaca, competing directly with Elysia for recognition as the primary successor, leading to prolonged territorial disputes along their shared frontier.

The three successor states, alongside numerous smaller polities that emerged in peripheral regions, engaged in intermittent warfare throughout the 6th and 7th centuries, known today as the **Triumvirate Wars**. The conflicts devastated formerly prosperous regions and disrupted the trade networks that had once sustained the Territoria Elysiaca. Ultimately, none of the successors possessed sufficient military or economic strength to achieve hegemony, resulting in a fractured political landscape that endured for centuries.

Over the following centuries, the successor states fragmented as central authority weakened and regional power centers asserted independence once again. By the 9th century, Elysia, Varennia, and Interlacria had each splintered into numerous smaller polities. Indigenous peoples successfully reasserted control over vast amounts of territories, particularly in interior and frontier regions beyond the reach of the core coastal Rumaic settlements. Low-intensity warfare continued, with all periodic attempts at reunification invariably collapsing.

### Evrian Settlement

In 1346 CE, the Black Death reached Evria through the Baishin Invasion, triggering a catastrophic demographic collapse across the continent. Seeking refuge from the pandemic, Evrians began migrating to Elysia en masse, establishing settlements across the Eastern section of the continent. Rumaic states, already weakened by centuries of fragmentation, were overwhelmed by the scale and speed of the migration. The remaining Rumaic-speaking populations were either assimilated into the incoming Evrian cultures or displaced, effectively ending the last remnants of Rumaic culture in Elysia. Most indigenous polities were eventually absorbed into the expanding settler states, but the Calverians maintained their independence.

One notable expedition by Elvesterians and Testuderans pushed farther southwest across the continent in the early 1500s. The Elvesterians halted at the southern edge of the Laurentian Plains, establishing the territory of Hollow's End (modern-day Hollosend) and its first city, Delvin, while the Testuderan contingent continued southward into Auritania, founding the colony of Navarelo in the tropical interior.

Simultaneously, Evrian merchants began sailing eastward across the Tethys Sea and Eastern Panthalassan Ocean to reach Elysia's western coast, establishing a pattern of transoceanic commerce. In 1650, the indigenous Penutian Empire negotiated a treaty with Suevia, a kingdom from the Holy Evrian Empire, ceding two ports to the Suevians in exchange for protection against other Evrian powers. This marked the establishment of the first Evrian colony in Western Elysia, which, after multiple shifts in leadership, evolved into the Reichskolonie Westelysien, the precursor to Ponurvia.

By 1800, the settler states had consolidated into mostly rigid polities, alongside two indigenous states and some remnants of colonial rule.

## Geography

### Topography

The Elysian continent can be broadly divided into five ‘great regions’: the geologically young, mountainous [Elysian Cordillera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Cordillera), including the [Assinwati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains) [Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains) and the [Great Ponurvian Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Basin); the central [Laurentian Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains) between the Assinwati Mountains and the Lannahechee River valley, a broad expanse of flatland stretching from the Gulf of Hollosend, north to the Arctic Sea, and east to the Great Lakes; the raised but relatively flat plateau of the [Varennian Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Shield) in the far north; the more varied **Old Elysia** east of the Lannahechee, which includes the [Apalchen Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Mountains), the coastal plain along the Saharan seaboard, and the numerous fertile river deltas of the Confederacy; and Auritania, a wide, volcanically active peninsula at the southern edge of the continent with dense tropical rainforests in the interior.

### Climate

Elysia experiences a diverse range of climates, though its equatorial regions are significantly colder than typical equatorial regions due to its position as a primary recipient of frigid polar air originating from [[hyperborea]]'s interior ice plateau, which counterbalances equatorial heating. This pattern is also evident across most of Evria, effectively pushing the meteorological equator and its associated tropical climate belt to a much lower latitude than the geographical equator, displacing the highest heat zones farther south.

Northern Elysia, comprising regions roughly on the same oblique latitude as the Varennian Shield, experiences the most pronounced cooling effect due to its direct exposure to these winds. Winter temperatures regularly drop to -25 to -35°C in continental interior regions, with coastal areas moderated to -15 to -20°C by maritime influence. Summer temperatures range from 15 to 25°C, creating a pronounced seasonal temperature swing of 40 to 50°C in some locations due to the extreme variation in Hyperborean wind intensity between winter (maximum katabatic activity) and summer (wind reversal period). Snow cover persists for 5-7 months annually, with permafrost present in the northernmost zones.

Central Elysia, comprising the northern Elysian Cordillera, Laurentian Plains, and Old Elysia, exhibits a far more complex combination of continental, maritime, and transitional climates, shaped by the interaction between Hyperborean air, Panthalassan maritime air masses, and the occasional influence of the equator. Eastern coastal regions experience humid continental conditions, with winters ranging from -10°C to -20°C and summers from 20 °C to 28°C. Western coastal areas exhibit comparatively temperate maritime characteristics, with mild, wet winters ranging from 0 to 10°C and cool, dry summers of 15 to 25°C, moderated by prevailing currents. The continental interior experiences extreme temperature ranges, with winter lows of -20 to -30°C and summer highs of 25 to 35°C, despite lying at latitudes that would typically support subtropical conditions on a terrestrial planet lacking Hyperborea's polar influence. Mountain ranges running through the Elysian Cordillera create pronounced orographic effects, with leeward areas experiencing rain shadow effects that produce semi-arid conditions with annual rainfall of less than 400 millimeters. The mountains also impede polar air penetration to the Panthalassan coast, creating milder winter conditions west of the ranges than at equivalent latitudes in the continental interior or along the eastern seaboard.

Southern Elysia gradually transitions from temperate to subtropical and tropical conditions as the polar wind diminishes with distance from the source. However, even these southern zones experience occasional air intrusions during winter months, bringing unseasonable cold snaps. Coastal areas along the southern margin and throughout Auritania exhibit true tropical conditions with year-round temperatures of 25 to 30°C and high humidity, while interior highlands maintain more temperate conditions.

## Government

The current-day Elysia is predominantly composed of parliamentary democracies and constitutional democracies, with the [[unity-state]] standing out from the norm. The political scene of modern-day Elysia was primarily drawn during [[great-war|the Great War]], with subsequent colonial liberation and civil conflicts bringing rise to entirely new nations and forms of government.

The most prevalent political organization on the continent is the Elysian Doctrine, a treaty organization formed following the Great War to safeguard Elysian interests, both domestically and internationally, promote trade and cooperation between the different nations of Elysia, and create a common political forum to discuss matters pertinent to the continent of Elysia. The second most prevalent alliance on the continent was the [[mizuhara]] Pentapartite, with the Empire of Ponurvia being a member, before its dissolution in 1931.
